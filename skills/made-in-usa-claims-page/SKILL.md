# American manufacturing / Made in USA page

Pair this with `client-question-rounds` (how to ask) and `marketing-compliance-gate` (pre-publish check). **Check thresholds against current official sources (FTC, OMB/FAR) before quoting them to a client.** The figures below were correct in Oct 2026.

## Claim ladder (strictest first)
| Claim | Bar |
|---|---|
| "Made in USA" (unqualified) | FTC: "all or virtually all" U.S., with negligible foreign content. The FTC looks at raw materials and key parts too, so an imported bearing or foreign pig iron in castings can break it. **"American-made" and "Made in America" count the same as "Made in USA."** |
| "Made in USA with U.S. and imported parts" | Qualified. Clients often dislike the phrase "imported parts". Ask them before using it. |
| "Assembled in USA" | Substantial transformation in final assembly here. |
| Buy American Act (federal purchasing) | Made in the U.S. with 65%+ domestic component cost (rising to 75% in 2029). Components count by where the component is made, not where its raw material came from. |
| BABA (federally funded infrastructure) | Manufactured products: more than 55% domestic component cost. Products that are mainly iron or steel: 100% U.S. melting and manufacturing. Confirm which class the product falls in before claiming eligibility. |
| Company-level: "U.S. manufacturer", "American manufacturer", plus specifics | Describes the company, not product origin. This is usually the safe, strong default, and it's what AI search quotes. |

**Default direction:** lead with company-level language and specifics: "engineered, manufactured and tested in <City, ST>, with the support of a network of U.S. suppliers for <castings, heat treat, specialty machining>". Product-level "Made in USA" only for configurations the client verifies, including raw-material origin. Handle BABA and Buy American per project through the normal quote/RFQ flow.

## What to ask (at most 2-3, after researching their materials)
1. Which products or configurations, if any, are essentially all U.S.-made, and are their castings or raw materials U.S.-origin? Offer to leave that section off at launch.
2. Are you OK with us showing the supply chain by service and location? Promise vendor names will stay off.
3. Anything your own materials and notes can't settle (which steps happen in-house, for example). Write it as a confirm question.
Ownership, BABA request routing and footer/homepage copy are usually the account lead's call. Ask them first.

## Page outline (~9 sections, from the Oct 2026 industrial manufacturer reference)
1. Hero with a quotable short answer (company-level claim + city + supplier network) and a fact strip (since <year> · <city>: what's done in-house · partner states · ISO cert)
2. History timeline (founding year, not "110+ years"; use the client's patent and archive images)
3. What happens in <city> (in-house processes, testing)
4. Built with U.S. partners: cards by **service + city** (no vendor names), a real map of the partner states, outside shops labeled "specialty"/support
5. What it means for buyers (lead times, existing designs, parts made to order, from their own brochure)
6. Quality (ISO 9001 registrar, AGMA, the standards they design to)
7. BABA / Buy American documentation: route through the existing quote and contact forms, plus a click-to-call phone link
8. FAQ (6 answers, open on the page, mirrored in FAQ schema). Include "Is <brand> an American company?", which needs ownership as well as location.
9. CTA. Then sitewide: footer banner links to the page, plus a Quick Links entry; internal links from home, About, product and repair pages; Organization schema with the street address.
Title pattern: "U.S. <Product> Manufacturer Since <Year> | <Brand>, <City, ST>".

## Wording gotchas
- Use the client's exact preferred phrase ("U.S. manufacturer" rather than "USA-based" in the reference case) everywhere: hero, title tag, H2s, CTA, FAQ.
- Leave out "U.S. first-tier supply chain" and steel or material distributors when the upstream origin is unknown. Listing them as U.S. invites the question.
- A partner list can make an in-house claim look false, but don't drop the claim until the client confirms. In the reference case, outside milling and grinding were *specialty* work, and the main machining and gear hobbing were in-house.
- Don't imply every unit of a component is domestic ("bearings made in <City, ST> for qualifying configurations").
- Never name suppliers on the page. They change, and they may compete for the client's work.
- An unqualified "Proudly Made in the USA" in an existing footer is a risk. Raise it **once**. If the account lead or the client keeps it, record that and don't bring it up again.
- No contrast framing in the copy (house rule).
